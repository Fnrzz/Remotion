# Character Sheet — Anime Male with Glasses

## 1. Purpose
This document is a character reference guide for AI image and video generation, animation, motion graphics, and storyboarding. Use the **pose IDs** to locate the correct panel in the reference image and preserve the character's visual identity. Emotion labels describe visible expressions, not verified internal feelings.

## 2. Grid Layout and Position Rules
- Reference sheet: **5 columns × 4 rows**, totaling **20 panels**.
- Read panels **left to right, then top to bottom**.
- `R1C1` means row 1, column 1; `R4C5` means row 4, column 5.
- Panel numbers: row 1 = 01–05; row 2 = 06–10; row 3 = 11–15; row 4 = 16–20.
- Left and right describe **directions within the image from the viewer's perspective**, not necessarily the character's anatomical left or right.

## 3. Character Identity — Keep Consistent
- **Character:** young adult male rendered in semi-realistic anime / cel-shaded illustration style.
- **Hair:** thick, tousled, wavy-to-curly black hair with bangs falling over the forehead.
- **Glasses:** translucent/light-gray rectangular frames with softly rounded corners.
- **Face:** defined jawline, warm skin tone, dark eyes, expressive eyebrows.
- **Outfit:** black/charcoal T-shirt with a dark neck strap or lanyard.
- **Framing:** primarily bust portraits (head, neck, shoulders, upper chest); hands appear in selected poses.
- **Do not change:** hairstyle or color, glasses shape, facial proportions, clothing, or art style unless explicitly instructed.

## 5. Detailed Panel Descriptions

### Row 1

#### 01 — R1C1 — Neutral / Serious
- **Head and body position:** head slightly tilted and turned to a three-quarter angle; gaze directed forward and slightly sideways.
- **Facial expression:** closed lips, relatively relaxed eyebrows, serious eyes.
- **Conveyed emotion:** neutral, composed, slightly aloof.
- **Best used for:** listening, introductions, understated reactions.

#### 02 — R1C2 — Laughing / Joyful
- **Head and body position:** head tilted back and to the side; mouth wide open.
- **Facial expression:** closed eyes, broad open-mouth laugh with teeth visible.
- **Conveyed emotion:** delight, amusement, carefree laughter.
- **Best used for:** jokes, humorous reveals, funny reactions.

#### 03 — R1C3 — Friendly Smile
- **Head and body position:** almost front-facing, with a slight head tilt.
- **Facial expression:** subtle smile, relaxed focused eyes.
- **Conveyed emotion:** friendly, confident, optimistic.
- **Best used for:** greetings, explanations, introducing a topic.

#### 04 — R1C4 — Shocked / Surprised
- **Head and body position:** upright head, facing the viewer directly.
- **Facial expression:** widened eyes, raised eyebrows, open O-shaped mouth.
- **Conveyed emotion:** shock, disbelief, astonishment.
- **Best used for:** surprising news, plot twists, unexpected facts.

#### 05 — R1C5 — Thinking / Curious
- **Head and body position:** face turned sideways, gaze directed upward and to the side; one hand supports the chin.
- **Facial expression:** distant focused gaze, closed lips.
- **Conveyed emotion:** curiosity, contemplation, consideration.
- **Best used for:** asking questions, weighing options, forming hypotheses.

## 6. Instructions for AI Models
1. Identify the requested panel using its exact ID, from `R1C1` through `R4C5`.
2. Choose **one pose ID** matching the target emotion and scene.
3. Treat the provided reference image as the **source of truth** for appearance, head direction, facial expression, and hand gesture.
4. Keep character identity consistent. Only modify motion, expression, or framing as explicitly requested for a scene.
5. For animation, start from the selected pose and use subtle motion as appropriate (blinking, head tilting, breathing, lip-sync) without changing the character's identity.
6. For transparent character assets, remove all backgrounds, panel backgrounds, and grid dividers. Keep hair, glasses, arms, and hands intact.
7. Do not combine gestures or expressions from different panels unless explicitly asked.
8. For left/right instructions, always follow the **viewer's perspective** defined in Section 2.
